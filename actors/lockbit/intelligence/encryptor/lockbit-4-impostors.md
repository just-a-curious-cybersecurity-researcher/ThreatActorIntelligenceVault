**Presentation reviewed:** 2026-09-22.

## Scope

Unit 42 published five hashes on 2024-02-24 under the explicit label possible LockBit 4.0 impostors. This page is an identification boundary for those artifacts, not an invented reverse engineering of their internals.

This page summarizes Unit 42's published indicator evidence. It does not claim independent execution or disassembly of the five samples. Bibliographic provenance is centralized in [References](../../References.md#executable-analysis-provenance).

## Executable Analysis

### Published artifact set

Unit 42 records five executable hashes and associated extortion artifacts. The supplied note identifies itself as a 2024 4.0 product and requests a fixed USD 1,000 payment through a specific Bitcoin address and email.

Those values support grouping the published artifact set. A low demand alone does not prove impersonation, and the report's “possible” qualification is retained.

### Attribution boundary

The existence of the leaked Black builder supplies a broader explanation for brand reuse, but it does not prove these five binaries were generated by that builder. Their provenance must not be inferred from temporal proximity to Cronos.

The [hash register](../../iocs/hashes.md) labels this set separately. Its associated address is recorded as a note payment artifact rather than an OFAC-attributed LockBit administrator address.

### Comparison with later releases

NG-Dev's .NET configuration and the native 2025 4.0 reverse engineering refer to different evidence. Neither should be used to populate missing internals for these five samples. The public indicator set is useful for historical exact matching and for preventing false organizational attribution.

## Linking Host Activity to the Executable

An exact hash match identifies one of the published possible-impostor files. It does not establish membership in the LockBit service. No unsupported service-stop, cryptographic or propagation routine is assigned to this set.
