# DragonForce — Blockchain and Financial Intelligence

**Presentation reviewed:** 2026-09-24.
**Last updated:** 2026-09-24  
**TLP:** CLEAR

## Overview

DragonForce is financially motivated and demands cryptocurrency. The earliest archived note explicitly requires BTC. Microsoft’s Defender description also records a Bitcoin demand. No public, source-attributed DragonForce wallet set was located in RansomLook, government notices, TRM, Chainalysis, Elliptic or the reviewed technical reports.

## Blockchain Attribution and Visibility Limitations

RansomLook’s DragonForce crypto page returned “No wallet found for this group” at the cutoff. Public ransom notes replace victim identifiers and payment details with redactions, so the shared note cannot expose a victim deposit address. A negotiation may generate a unique address; absence from open reporting does not mean that no payment addresses exist.

Code reuse does not transfer financial attribution. LockBit sanctions and wallets cannot be assigned to DragonForce because an early locker used the leaked LockBit builder. The same boundary applies to Conti-derived code and payments attributed to Scattered Spider or RansomHub.

## Financial Evidence Timeline

- **2024-01:** the archived note specifies BTC payment. This is direct payment-method evidence, but it exposes no address.
- **2024:** recruitment reporting describes an 80% affiliate share. This is a commercial claim; individual settlement mechanics remain private.
- **2025:** the cartel note says demand size is based on revenue and insurance, directly exposing the negotiation model.
- **2025:** TRM includes DragonForce among emerging ransomware services and notes leaked audio suggesting a Northern/Central Asian nexus; it publishes no wallet cluster.
- **2026-09-24:** RansomLook’s crypto register contains no DragonForce wallet. This confirms a public-data gap, not the absence of payments.

## Deviations from Phase IV

No victim-specific transaction could be reconstructed, so there is no defensible phase model for deposit, consolidation, conversion, bridge use or cash-out. General ransomware observations about mixers, cross-chain bridges and exchanges are not copied into an actor-specific flow without an address or transaction identifier.

## Financial Relationships

The RaaS model creates at least two potential beneficiary layers: affiliate and service. The reported 80/20 split suggests that the affiliate retained most proceeds in the 2024 program, but public reporting does not establish whether payments were automatically split on-chain, remitted later, or negotiated under different terms. White-label partners may use their own payment infrastructure.

No public blockchain evidence ties DragonForce wallets to LockBit, Qilin, RansomHub, Conti or Scattered Spider. Public operational relationships are documented elsewhere and must not be converted into financial clusters.

## Cash-Out Timing

No public DragonForce-attributed transaction set supports analysis of dwell time, exchange deposit timing, chain hopping or cash-out services.

## Intelligence Gaps

- Victim-specific BTC addresses and negotiation payment instructions.
- Transaction identifiers for confirmed payments.
- Affiliate/service split mechanics.
- Exchange, bridge, mixer or OTC exposure supported by traced flows.
- Addresses associated with the operator panel or DLS infrastructure.

## Analyst Note

The correct current finding is “payment in BTC is documented; attributable wallets and laundering routes are not public.” Claims that DragonForce probably uses a particular mixer or privacy coin are hypotheses until backed by an address, transaction or authoritative tracing report.

## Independent Corroboration and Financial-Service Disruption

TRM independently recognizes DragonForce as an emerging ransomware operation but does not publish a wallet cluster in the reviewed report. Chainalysis and Elliptic searches did not produce a DragonForce-specific public tracing report. RansomLook exposes no wallet.

No service disruption or seizure of DragonForce cryptocurrency was located. Law-enforcement action in the UK retail investigation concerned arrests and alleged offenses, not a published cryptocurrency forfeiture.

## Regulatory and Judicial Review

No direct DragonForce designation was found in the OFAC SDN program or reviewed U.S., UK or EU sanctions material as of 2026-09-24. No named DragonForce operator with a published forfeiture address was identified. Sanctions applying to other ransomware services are not inherited through code, affiliate or “cartel” associations.

## Multi-Chain Research and Next Collection Priorities

Collection should begin with verified victim negotiation records: the exact payment request, address, chain, amount, timestamp and transaction identifier. Those values can then be screened against sanctions data and traced across BTC services. ETH, XMR and cross-chain activity should be investigated only when case evidence names those assets; the public archive currently supports BTC.
