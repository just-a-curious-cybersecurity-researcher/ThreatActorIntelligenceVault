# Lynx — Blockchain and Financial Intelligence

**Presentation reviewed:** 2026-10-07.

## Overview

Lynx is a financially motivated extortion service, but public financial visibility is limited. Group-IB reports that affiliates provide the payment wallet, run negotiation and retain 80 percent, while the RaaS operator receives 20 percent. This decentralized collection model can fragment addresses across affiliates and victims.

No public Lynx-specific address was validated during this review. RansomLook's Lynx cryptocurrency page explicitly displayed no wallet. The absence of a public address corpus is a collection limitation, not evidence that cryptocurrency is unused.

## Blockchain Attribution and Visibility Limitations

Chainalysis reported links between INC and Lynx based on similar on-chain behavior and code overlap, but the public article did not disclose addresses or transaction graphs. That finding supports an ecosystem relationship; it cannot be independently converted into a blocklist or exact treasury path.

TRM Labs later described shared cash-out points connecting Kairos with SafePay, INC, Lynx and Qilin. TRM assessed that the overlap was more consistent with a shared affiliate than with one common operator. The public post did not expose the underlying addresses. This is useful relationship intelligence, but it does not support merging the five brands' wallet inventories.

An address shown in a ransom negotiation would establish a victim/payment context only after the note, chat and transaction are validated. It would not automatically identify the core operator because affiliates reportedly control payment wallets.

## Financial Evidence Timeline

- **2024:** Lynx emerges with a double-extortion model and affiliate-run negotiations.
- **2025:** Direct panel reporting documents the 80/20 split and affiliate-provided wallets.
- **2025:** TRM Labs reports shared cash-out points linking Lynx with Kairos, SafePay, INC and Qilin, assessing a likely shared affiliate; no addresses are published.
- **2026:** Chainalysis publicly describes INC/Lynx on-chain behavioral links without publishing addresses; RansomLook still lists no wallet.

## Deviations from Phase IV

There is no four-phase public laundering model comparable to the mature Akira research. Creating one would imply transaction visibility that is not available.

## Financial Relationships

The INC/Lynx relationship has two separate evidentiary bases: shared code and Chainalysis's undisclosed on-chain behavioral assessment. TRM's cash-out overlap adds a separate affiliate/ecosystem signal. None proves that all affiliate receipts consolidate into one Lynx treasury. Sinobi's technical relationship should not be extended to wallet attribution without transaction evidence.

## Cash-Out Timing

No public Lynx-specific cash-out interval, mixer route, bridge use or named exchange-deposit cluster was validated. TRM's shared cash-out finding establishes reuse of downstream collection points, but does not disclose whether the Lynx-linked flow arrived directly from a victim, through an affiliate wallet or after intermediate hops. The 20 percent operator share indicates an expected settlement step, but its timing and mechanism remain unknown.

TRM describes peel chains, co-spend clusters and higher-risk virtual-asset services in the Kairos cases surrounding that research. Those behaviors are not assigned to Lynx here because the public report does not state that the Lynx-linked flow followed each of those routes.

## Intelligence Gaps

- Victim payment addresses tied to dated negotiations.
- Affiliate-to-operator settlement transactions representing the reported 20 percent.
- Exchange, bridge, mixer or OTC service attributions with transaction evidence.
- Whether INC, Lynx and Sinobi share any treasury infrastructure rather than only behavioral patterns.

## Analyst Note

Do not manufacture financial indicators from 64-hex malware hashes, Tor identifiers or wallet addresses attributed only to INC. Preserve the source's exact scope and date.

## Independent Corroboration and Financial-Service Disruption

Chainalysis and TRM Labs independently make Lynx-specific relationship statements, but neither publishes a Lynx address or transaction graph. No public address-level corroboration from Elliptic was located.

## Regulatory and Judicial Review

No Lynx-specific OFAC SDN designation, Treasury press release, DOJ forfeiture address or public seizure was validated through 2026-10-07. This is not sanctions clearance: screen counterparties and addresses against current official lists at decision time.

## Multi-Chain Research and Next Collection Priorities

Priority collection is a dated negotiation wallet with payment evidence. From there, examine change behavior, consolidation, affiliate/operator splits, service deposits and cross-chain movement. A second priority is the undisclosed TRM cash-out cluster: publication of even one anchored address could test whether the overlap represents one affiliate, a negotiator, a broker or a shared laundering service. Until then, brand-level payment estimates should remain separate from traced receipts.
